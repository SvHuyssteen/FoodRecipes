# Hake Katsu

**Category:** Main · **Serves:** 2 · **Calories:** ~450 kcal per serve · **Protein:** ~35g
**Tags:** main, fish, hake, japanese, katsu, panko, air-fryer, quick, freezer-friendly

> The weekly fish. Hake is too lean and delicate to glaze — so don't fight it, crumb it. Frozen
> fillets make this a permanent freezer-to-table option.

## Ingredients

- 2 hake fillets (~180g each), fresh or fully thawed, patted VERY dry
- 30g flour, seasoned with salt and pepper
- 1 egg, beaten
- 50g panko breadcrumbs
- Oil spray
- Salt

### Quick tonkatsu-ish sauce
- 20ml Worcestershire sauce
- 15ml tomato ketchup
- 5ml soy sauce
- Pinch of sugar or allulose

### To serve
- 1/4 cabbage, shredded as fine as you can (the Kenwood slicer disc earns its keep)
- Lemon wedges

## Method

1. Stir the sauce together. Shred the cabbage and dress it with nothing but a squeeze of lemon.
2. Dry the fillets aggressively with paper towel — wet fish means soggy crumb. Flour, then egg, then panko, pressing the crumbs on firmly.
3. Spray both sides generously with oil (dry panko air-fries pale and dusty; oiled panko goes golden).
4. Air fry at 200C for 10-12 minutes on the static rack, flipping carefully at halfway, until deep golden and the fish flakes.
5. Rest 2 minutes, slice into strips katsu-style, salt lightly. Serve on the cabbage pile with the sauce and lemon.

## Notes

- The cabbage is not garnish — the mountain of lemony shredded cabbage against hot crumbed fish IS the dish. Volume food, basically free.
- Works identically with chicken breast (butterflied) when the freezer has no fish — same crumb, same sauce, ~480 kcal.
- Double-crumb raw fillets and freeze flat: cook from frozen at 190C, +4-5 minutes.
