# Instant Pot Lamb & Pea Soup with Orzo

**Category:** Main · **Serves:** 4 · **Calories:** ~520 kcal per serve · **Protein:** ~38g
**Tags:** main, lamb, soup, instant-pot, orzo, pea, corn, comfort, make-ahead, parmesan

## Ingredients

### Pressure cook
- 800g bone-in lamb (shoulder chops or neck)
- 1 large onion, roughly chopped
- 4 garlic cloves, crushed
- 30ml tomato paste
- 400g tin chopped tomatoes
- 500ml lamb or beef stock
- 2 bay leaves
- 3 sprigs rosemary or thyme
- 1 parmesan rind
- Salt and black pepper

### After pressure cook
- 200g frozen peas
- 200g corn kernels (tinned or frozen)
- Salt and pepper to taste

### For serving (per portion)
- 60g orzo per person
- Ladleful of soup broth to cook it in
- 15-20g parmesan, freshly grated, per bowl
- Fresh parsley to finish (optional)

## Method

1. Set Instant Pot to saute. Brown the lamb pieces in batches with a little oil — don't skip this, it builds the base flavour of the whole soup. Set aside.
2. In the same pot saute onion until soft, 3-4 minutes. Add garlic and tomato paste, cook 1-2 minutes until the paste darkens slightly.
3. Add tinned tomatoes, stock, bay leaves, rosemary, parmesan rind, salt and pepper. Return lamb to the pot. Seal and pressure cook on high for 40 minutes. Natural release for 10 minutes, then quick release.
4. Remove lamb pieces to a board. Pull all meat off the bones — it should fall away easily. Discard bones, large fat pieces, and the parmesan rind. Return meat to the pot.
5. Add peas and corn. Set to saute and simmer 15-20 minutes until vegetables are cooked through and broth has reduced slightly. Taste and adjust seasoning.
6. To serve: ladle the portion of soup you want for the evening into a separate smaller pot. Bring to a gentle boil, add orzo (60g per person) and cook 8-10 minutes until just tender. Serve immediately with freshly grated parmesan over each bowl.

## Notes

- Two-stage parmesan: rind into the broth for depth during the simmer, freshly grated on top per bowl for richness and finish. Both together is the move.
- Keeping the orzo separate is essential — it soaks up the broth overnight and turns to mush if stored together. Always cook orzo fresh per serving in a ladled-out portion.
- Bone-in lamb is essential — the bones enrich the broth significantly during pressure cooking.
- The soup base (without orzo) keeps well in the fridge 4-5 days and freezes beautifully.
- Woolworths lamb neck or shoulder chops work perfectly here.
- Woolworths sells parmesan wedges and pre-grated — either works.
