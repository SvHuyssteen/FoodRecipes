# Miso-Glazed Snoek

**Category:** Main · **Serves:** 4 · **Calories:** ~400 kcal per serve · **Protein:** ~40g
**Tags:** main, fish, snoek, japanese, south-african, miso, braai, omega-3, high-protein

> The sleeper: if salmon's health case is the omega-3s, snoek is SA's affordable answer — and
> the apricot-snoek braai tradition proves sweet glaze on oily fish works. This is the same
> idea speaking Japanese.

## Ingredients

- 1 butterflied snoek (~800g-1kg), bones in — ask the fishmonger to butterfly it
- 1 full batch [All-Purpose Miso Glaze](../sauces/miso-glaze.md)
- 5ml neutral oil
- 1 lemon
- Spring onion and sesame seeds to finish

## Method

1. Pat the snoek dry. Brush the flesh side lightly with oil.
2. **Vortex route:** skin side down on a foil-lined tray (cut to fit if needed), 190C for 8-10 minutes until nearly cooked. Brush generously with glaze and finish 3-4 minutes at 200C until bubbling and caught dark in spots.
3. **Braai route (the better one):** flesh side down over medium coals 4-5 minutes, flip to skin side, brush with glaze and cook 6-8 minutes more, glazing twice, until the thickest part flakes. Snoek is braai royalty — the smoke belongs in this dish.
4. Squeeze lemon over, scatter spring onion and sesame. Serve straight from the bone.

## Notes

- Glaze late — same rule as always; miso and honey burn before oily fish cooks.
- Snoek has bones; eating around them is part of the deal, same as the apricot version.
- Watch for pin-boniness and don't overcook — oily fish goes from lush to dry fast. Flaking at the thickest point means off the heat NOW.
- The most nutritious fish of the trio and usually the cheapest per portion — when you see fresh snoek, this is the plan.
- Leftovers flake into a brilliant smoorsnoek-style breakfast or over rice with the soy-vinegar sauce.
