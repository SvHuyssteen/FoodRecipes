# Peri-Peri Pork Loin with Charred Miso Cabbage & Sesame Cauliflower Rice

## Ingredients

### Peri-peri pork loin
- 800g pork loin, trimmed of surface fat
- 3 garlic cloves, finely grated
- 15ml peri-peri sauce (Woolworths habanero peri peri works) plus extra to serve
- 5ml smoked paprika
- 5ml dried oregano
- 2ml chilli flakes (or 1 African bird's eye chilli, finely chopped)
- 15ml red wine vinegar
- 15ml olive oil
- Juice and zest of half a lemon
- 8g salt, 3g black pepper

### Charred miso cabbage
- 1 small green or sweetheart cabbage (~600g), cut into 4 wedges through the core
- 15g white miso paste
- 10g butter, softened
- 5ml honey
- 5ml rice vinegar
- Oil spray

### Sesame cauliflower rice
- 1 medium cauliflower (~700g), riced in the Kenwood food processor
- 10ml sesame oil
- 2 spring onions, sliced
- 1 garlic clove, finely grated
- 10g toasted sesame seeds
- Salt to taste

## Method

1. Marinate the pork: mix garlic, peri-peri sauce, paprika, oregano, chilli, vinegar, olive oil, lemon juice and zest, salt and pepper into a loose paste. Coat the loin thoroughly. At least 1 hour at room temperature, or up to overnight in the fridge.
2. Preheat the air fryer oven to 190C. Roast the loin on the static rack 25-35 minutes, turning once, until internal temp hits 62C at the thickest point. Rest 8-10 minutes under foil — carryover takes it to ~65C and keeps it blush-pink and juicy.
3. While the pork cooks, mash miso, butter and honey together with the rice vinegar. Spray the cabbage wedges with oil and smear the miso butter into the cut faces and between the leaves.
4. When the pork comes out to rest, crank the air fryer to 200C and char the cabbage wedges 10-12 minutes until the edges blacken and the core is just tender. (Alternatively: a ripping-hot cast iron pan, cut side down, lid on for the last 3 minutes.)
5. Cauliflower rice: heat sesame oil in a wide pan over medium-high. Add garlic and the white parts of the spring onions, 30 seconds. Add the riced cauliflower and stir-fry 4-5 minutes — it should steam dry and just start to catch, not go mushy. Season with salt, finish with sesame seeds and the green spring onion tops.
6. Slice the rested pork thickly against the grain. Plate on the cauliflower rice with a cabbage wedge alongside; spoon resting juices and extra peri-peri over the pork.

## Notes

- Pull the pork at 62C — loin is lean and the line between juicy and dry is about 5 degrees. Use a meat thermometer.
- Don't skip the rest; an unrested loin dumps its juices on the board.
- The miso butter chars sweet-savoury rather than burnt — same register as the gochujang dishes, and it makes cabbage taste like the best part of a braai.
- Rice the cauliflower in the Kenwood processor; don't over-process to paste — pulse to couscous-size.
- Lowest-calorie main in the library alongside the pork chops — a default weeknight pick.
- Leftover pork slices are excellent cold in a wrap with yoghurt flatbreads and peri-peri mayo.
