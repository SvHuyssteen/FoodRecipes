# Roasted Garlic & Cream Cheese Cauliflower Mash

**Category:** Side · **Serves:** 4 · **Calories:** ~120 kcal per serve · **Protein:** ~6g
**Tags:** side, cauliflower, mash, low-cal, vegetarian, roasted-garlic, cream-cheese

## Ingredients

- 1 large cauliflower (~900g), cut into florets
- 1 whole garlic bulb
- 60g cream cheese
- 30g parmesan, finely grated
- 15ml olive oil
- 15g butter
- Salt and white pepper
- Fresh chives or parsley to finish (optional)

## Method

1. Roast the garlic first. Slice the top off the whole bulb to expose the cloves, drizzle with olive oil, wrap in foil and roast at 200C for 35-40 minutes until completely soft and golden. Squeeze the roasted cloves out — they should pop out easily. Set aside.
2. Steam the cauliflower florets over boiling water for 10-12 minutes until completely tender — a knife should meet zero resistance. Don't boil directly in water or the mash will be watery.
3. Transfer steamed cauliflower to a clean tea towel or spread on a tray and leave 3-4 minutes to steam dry. Even steamed cauliflower holds water — skipping this gives a loose, watery mash. Alternatively use a salad spinner to remove excess moisture efficiently.
4. Transfer to food processor. Add roasted garlic cloves, cream cheese, butter, parmesan, salt and white pepper. Blitz until completely smooth and silky — scrape down the sides and blitz again. Taste and adjust seasoning aggressively — cauliflower needs more salt than you think.
5. Serve immediately topped with fresh chives or parsley and a crack of black pepper.

## Notes

- Roasting the garlic is what makes this — raw or pan-fried garlic is too sharp. Roasted garlic is sweet, mellow and nutty.
- White pepper keeps it visually clean — black pepper leaves specks.
- Fat-free Greek yoghurt can replace some or all of the cream cheese to trim calories further.
- Salad spinner works brilliantly for removing moisture — spin after salting for best results.
- Reheat with a splash of milk or stock — it thickens significantly in the fridge overnight.
- Pairs brilliantly with Middle Eastern Pork Chops, Peri-Peri Pork Loin, Rotisserie Chicken, Sirloin Steak, or as a base for the Bold Pepper Steak Pie.
